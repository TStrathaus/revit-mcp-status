Modelling guideline: as-built Revit models (scan2revit)

These are the rules a model made with scan2revit follows, and the ones it should follow where the code does not yet (marked open). The workflow (running the pipeline, mapping types, sending the calls) is in skills/scan-to-revit/SKILL.md; this file says what the result must be. G-0.1 When a project has its own BIM execution plan (BAP, SIA 2051) or the client's modelling rules, those come first; this guideline fills what they leave open.

G-0.2 Data first, then the modelling decision. Before anything is modelled, the available data is analysed (input reports of the scans, IFC and plans; drawings such as sections and build-up legends; documentation), and the level of detail per element class is decided from it and recorded (the project manager at gate 1, in status.md: "Modelling decisions"). The simple form is the default: a slab is one element of its full thickness (finished floor to soffit), a wall one element face to face. A slab is split into the structural slab and its floor build-up, or a wall into its layers, only where sections, build-up legends or documentation give that build-up (accepted facts, documentation/drawings-and-scans.md), never because a template or a habit offers it. (User, 25.10.2026.) The analysis looks first for the construction: materials, structure, thicknesses and layers of slabs and walls (user, 26.10.2026).

G-0.3 (proposed 26.10.2026, awaiting approval) Sources derived from each other are one source. Drawings or models made from the scan carry the scan's values: they are used as information (names, units) but never to verify the scan. Every source of a project says where it comes from (design, construction, as-built survey, derived from the scan); the checker compares only independent sources.

G-0.4 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) The scan's quality is checked before anything is modelled (Valero et al. 2022, rules 3, 5, 9, 10): (a) registration: where scans overlap, a wall seen as two parallel faces 1 to 3 cm apart is a registration error, reported with its place, never modelled as two walls or a thick wall; (b) ghost points behind mirrors, glass and polished surfaces and points of moving people are removed or reported before extraction; (c) coverage per room: the share of its wall length and floor the scan sees; what is not seen is "not verifiable", not "wrong". The input report and the analysis page show all three.

G-0.5 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) The tolerance is agreed at gate 1 and recorded in status.md: a USIBD LOA class (LOA20 15-50 mm, LOA30 5-15 mm, LOA40 1-5 mm, measured and represented apart) or a class of 60 / 30 / 15 mm (Bonduel et al. 2017). The input report compares the scan with it (point spacing, 3D position; a typical specification for building scans is point spacing up to 5 mm and 3D position up to 7 mm, Autodesk Tandem guidance v4.2). Without an agreement the default is LOA20 for the model (represented), and the report says so.

Every rule has a stable id (G-1.1, G-2.1, ...: section and number, in bold before the rule). Checks, findings and rule proposals name the rule by its id; which check tests which rule: section 14. A rule changes only when the user approved the change (proposals: documentation/agent-team.md); a new rule takes the next free number of its section, a removed rule's id is not used again.

Basis: the BIMForum Level of Development specification (LOD 200: generic elements at the right size and place), the USIBD Level of Accuracy (LOA: measured and represented accuracy kept apart), SIA 2051 / KBOB (the level of detail is set per project in the BAP), the SBB rules for building models (an as-built model holds existing elements only), the Autodesk Revit documentation (constraints, room bounding, location line, shared coordinates). No published Swiss modelling guideline for as-built models from scans was found; sources at the end.

1. Walls and slabs are generic, no construction information inside

  • G-1.1 Every wall and every floor (slab) has a generic type: one layer, no material, no build-up. The type carries the thickness and nothing else; its name says so: scan2revit 300 mm, scan2revit floor 400 mm.
  • Why: a scanner sees surfaces, not what is behind them; a plan or a scan does not show the build-up. A layered type from a template (plaster, brick, insulation) states a construction nobody measured. BIMForum LOD 200 is exactly this: a generic assembly at the right size, shape and place. Single-layer components are also what modelling guides ask for when the build-up is not known (bimaterial, variant MGPa).
  • G-1.2 The build-up is added later, by the project, when it is known: by swapping the generic type for a project type of the same thickness, never by editing the generic one.
  • G-1.3 Doors and windows follow the same idea: generic families with free width and height; no fire rating, no hardware, no material.
  • G-1.4 Current: the Revit module (revit_runtime.py, run by pyRevit) uses only scan2revit wall and floor types (one layer, no material) and makes the missing ones (floor types are copied from a floor type, never from a foundation slab type, which Revit also files as a floor type); the check "walls and floors generic" fails on any wall or floor with a build-up or a material. All nine examples passed it in Revit 2025, through the old add-in (0.5) and through pyRevit (0.7, 20.10.2026). Door and window sizes are set where the family takes them: Swiss families ("TU ...") through "Angabe Maueröffnungsmaß Breite / Höhe" (the masonry opening, what a scan measures), read back from "Breite" / "Höhe ab FBOK"; every door and window of the examples gets its size. A door of the plan never gets a passage family (an opening without a leaf, e.g. "TU Durchgang": it draws no swing in plan), even where its stock size fits best; a passage of the plan gets no element but a room separation line (section 9). The template's door families still carry fire ratings in some types (open: generic door and window families without them in blank.rvt).
  • G-1.5 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) Building elements are never Generic Models or DirectShapes: every wall, floor, column, stair, door and window is an element of its own Revit category and type (Autodesk Tandem guidance). DirectShapes only for what helps to look at the scan (the scan layers Scan Lage n), in Generic Models and named so.
  • G-1.6 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) A door or a window is one element: frame, leaf and glass together (Autodesk Tandem guidance; with G-1.3).

2. Measured or assumed: say which

  • G-2.1 Every dimension is either measured or assumed, and the model says which. Assumed are today: the thickness of a wall seen from one side (thickness_assumed), door head, window sill and head in the slice route and in 2D plans (heights_assumed), slab thickness without a vertical section through it.
  • G-2.2 The accuracy is stated per element class as USIBD LOA: measured accuracy (of the scan) and represented accuracy (of the model) apart. Measured on real data so far: real project data (details private); synthetic faces within 1 mm.
  • G-2.3 In Revit: the shared instance parameter scan2revit source (group Data) on walls, floors, doors and windows says measured (taken from the scan, the drawing or the IFC) or assumed: <what> (assumed: thickness, assumed: head, assumed: sill and head). The last build stage (existing) adds it through a shared parameter file in the temp folder and restores the user's own shared parameter file setting. The flags stay in plan.json and report.md as well.
  • G-2.4 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) Once the deviation analysis exists (F25): every wall, floor, column, door and window carries its deviation from the scan as an instance parameter scan2revit deviation (within <class>, outside <class>: <mm>, not verifiable), so the model shows where it may be trusted (Bonduel et al. 2017: non-compliant elements flagged in the model).

3. Existing elements only

  • G-3.1 An as-built model holds the building as it is: no demolition, no new construction, no design intent.
  • G-3.2 Every element in the phase "Existing" (Bestand): the stage existing sets "Phase Created" of every element the tool made to the template's existing phase (by name: Existing, Bestand, Bestehend; else the first phase). Rooms keep the phase of the view they were placed in; they are bounded by the existing walls all the same.
  • G-3.3 Not modelled, only reported in the QA: furniture, fittings, MEP, finishes, build-ups, anything the scan only shows in part (ghost points, mirrors).

4. Levels and storeys

  • G-4.1 One level per floor top (finished floor as scanned), named by its storey (EG, 1OG, ... or as the project names them). A gallery (a partial floor with less than 2.2 m headroom) gets a level of its own, Gallery n.
  • G-4.2 Every element sits on the level of its storey; nothing hangs on a level of another storey.
  • G-4.3 Elevations: Level 0 at elevation 0 in the local frame; the survey offset carries the height above sea (plan.origin.offset_xyz). Several storeys from one survey frame (merge) keep their measured heights.
  • G-4.4 Floor heights come from the vertical sections (scan2revit.pointcloud.sections); the scan's height histogram only where no section can be cut. A slab underside (a ceiling with a floor 0.12-0.9 m above it) is never a floor. Every check made at a floor height (density, registration) uses these floors. Storeys with steps are separate levels or reported (steps_to in slabs.json). (Approved by the user 25.10.2026, proposed by the point cloud man.)
  • G-4.5 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) An element spanning levels (stair, shaft, a wall of a double-height room) has its base on the lower level and its top tied to the upper one; it is listed once, on its base level (Autodesk Tandem guidance).

5. Walls

  • G-5.1 Straight walls on their centreline (location line "Wall Centerline", the same as "Core Centerline" for a single-layer generic wall), from face to face as measured. Curved walls as arcs (circular only).
  • G-5.2 Base: the storey level. Top: the underside of the slab above, set as a constraint, not as a height (user, 25.11.2026): top constraint "up to level" = the level above, top offset = minus the slab's thickness, so the wall follows that level when it moves. Walls stop at the slabs; they never run through them. A wall the input carries over several storeys is split at each slab (the part above becomes a wall of that storey, unless one stands there); a wall with no floor above it (a double-height room, a gallery) stays whole; a wall is never cut below the head of an opening in it. Walls that do not reach the slab keep their unconnected height (core.merge.cap_walls). Construction (user, 25.11.2026): walls stand on the slab of their storey (a base offset up to 15 cm is set to 0) and reach the underside of the slab above (a top up to 30 cm below it is tied to it), so the walls of one area of a storey have one height, set only by the slab above; different heights only where the slabs above lie at different heights, or for walls that are really low (parapets, knee walls).
  • G-5.3 Corners and junctions: centrelines joined at their crossings; overlapping walls are an error to remove.
  • G-5.4 Room bounding: on (the default) for every wall.
  • G-5.5 A wall seen from one side keeps its seen face; the thickness is assumed (0.30 m unless --exterior-thickness) until an external scan (facade or drone, same survey frame: scan2revit.pointcloud.facade) shows its outer face, a door reveal measures it or --thickness-overrides sets it. The outer face is the dominant face 8 cm to 1 m behind the inner one in the storey's height band (0.30-1.60 m); pilaster fronts and window frames are smaller peaks. Then the wall is "paired", thickness source "inner face + exterior scan". Not for a storey under a sloping roof or below ground (--no-walls): the band sees eaves and dormers, or terrain.
  • G-5.7 (user, 25.11.2026) Linings, finishes and objects are not walls: a thin piece (up to 60 mm) the input carries as a wall in front of a wall becomes part of that wall, face to face, its closing pieces are left out (ifc.linings); in a scan, a thin short "wall" just in front of a longer wall is an object on it (a picture, a TV, a cupboard) and is left out (slice2d.objects_on_walls).
  • G-5.6 Orientation: with --align-north the model is turned so the walls run along the project axes (project north); true north keeps the survey bearing (plan.origin.rotation_deg).
  • G-5.8 (user, 28.11.2026) Wall tops by area when no storey above is scanned: each wall reaches the ceiling measured beside it (0.15 to 1.2 m from its faces, the higher side), snapped to the storey's area ceilings (the peaks of the flat ceiling heights, within 8 cm); never above the storey's ceiling. A ceiling seen 0.25 m or more below the storey's ceiling is a suspended ceiling: reported, not built; a wall that sees only it takes the ceiling of the walls within 3 m (slice2d.local_ceilings, qa.local_ceilings).
  • G-5.9 (user, 28.11.2026) A wall measured from both faces keeps its measured thickness, even a thin one (a facade of 58 mm: lining or glazing); no assumed facade is added behind it, and the report names it. G-5.7 still applies where a thin piece stands in front of another wall.

6. Floors (slabs) and voids

  • G-6.1 One floor per slab region (from a horizontal slice at the level, scan2revit.pointcloud.sections / slice2d --slabs), top of the slab at the level, thickness downwards as measured in the vertical sections (else 0.25 m, assumed). Without slab regions the outline comes from the walls or the free space.
  • G-6.2 Voids are openings cut into the floor (NewOpening): shafts and stairwells (5 m2 or more with no ceiling of the storey below under them), courtyards and atria (over 60 m2). Holes the floor is only hidden in (furniture) are not voids. (User, 25.11.2026) Shafts (building services) and stairwells run vertically through the building: a hole is a shaft only where it lies at the same place on two floors or more, and it is then cut in every floor from the lowest one up; a hole on one floor only is a scan shadow. A courtyard or atrium is one rectangle through all floors, its sides on the walls around it; otherwise there is a floor.
  • G-6.3 With an external scan the floor outline is the building's outline at the facade (pointcloud.facade): the vertical facade points around the storey form a ring (windows and doors closed, up to 1.6 m; 2.4 and 3.2 m only where the ring stays open); inside the outer ring is slab, also where the inside was not scanned; an enclosed space of 30 m2 or more with no scanned floor and its own ring of facade points is a courtyard (a void). Flat things in front of the facade (canopies, terrace roofs, sills) are not facade; protrusions under 0.8 m (pilasters) are ironed out; edges near the building axes are squared. The edge lies on the outer face of the load-bearing walls: the outline is pulled in by the cladding depth, measured as the median distance from the outer face of the walls measured from the external scan to the facade outline (cladding, cornices, sills are not slab). A ring that does not hold 90 % of the scanned floor is open (missing points, shop fronts, terrain): the scanned outline stays.
  • G-6.4 (user, 25.11.2026) Floors are straight and square: the outline follows the building axes, ends on the axis of the exterior wall (middle of the wall), steps and notches under 1 m are ironed out; where the facade ring is open the storey's walls still give the floor's extent. Openings only where the building has them: shafts (building services), stairwells, the courtyard; a shaft is a rectangle on the faces of its walls, holes under 1 m2 or outside the floor are scan shadows and not cut. The sections give the floor's height, thickness and openings, not its edge (slice2d.square_floors). Also the slab above the storey (its floor, from the sections). (open: curved facades as arcs; a storey edge the scan does not show stays recessed.)
  • G-6.5 (proposed 26.10.2026, awaiting approval) A slab never reaches beyond the building: one scan that holds inside and outside together (a registered cloud with the facade, terrace and garden) is split into inside and outside points by the exterior walls first, and the outside points are the external scan of G-6.3. Floor points outside the exterior walls (terrace, terrain, a neighbour's yard at the same height) are not slab; a balcony or terrace slab is its own element and is listed, not merged into the storey's floor. Slice route (slice2d.building_outline): the building outline is the wall bodies and the indoor floor (floor and ceiling seen; a terrace has no ceiling) closed over gaps up to 1.6 m, protrusions under 0.8 m ironed out, straightened and squared to the building axes; the slice points beyond it measure the walls seen from inside (G-6.3); rooms and voids mostly outside it are dropped. (open: a balcony or terrace slab of its own.)

7. Doors and windows

  • G-7.1 Hosted in their wall, one generic type per size, named scan2revit <w> x <h> (a copy of the nearest type with width and height set; existing sizes within 50 mm are reused). Sizes under 200 mm are not openings.
  • G-7.2 Width and position as measured; sill and head as measured in the full scan route, given (and flagged) in the slice route and in 2D plans.
  • G-7.3 Door swing and hinge side as scanned (the open leaf), drawn (the swing arc) or given in the IFC (single-swing doors: the leaf opens towards the door's local +Y axis, the hinge side from the operation type LEFT / RIGHT); a closed door in a scan and a double door in an IFC have none: the swing stays the family default and is reported.
  • G-7.5 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) A curtain, blind or screen in front of a window is not a wall: a flat surface within 0.3 m inside a facade wall, parallel to it and no wider than the opening behind, is dropped; the window behind it is reported as doubtful (Valero et al. 2022, rule 4).
  • G-7.4 (user, 28.11.2026) Openings found in a wall's elevation with a measured sill and head (clerestories, narrow openings high in the wall from 0.40 m wide) are built as windows of their measured size, not left out as ribs or vents; inner ones take the fixed-glazing family where the template has it (family_hint).

8. Stairs, slab openings, ramps

  • G-8.1 (user, 25.11.2026) Stairs are not built: a stair found in the scan becomes its stairwell, a rectangle around its runs and landings cut from the floor above, and its landings, floors at their height (0.20 m, assumed). The runs, risers and tread are reported. (Before: one straight run per flight in a StairsEditScope; the runs came out through the slab above and without landings.)
  • G-8.2 Ramps and sloped roofs are reported, not built.
  • G-8.3 (proposed 26.10.2026, awaiting approval) Columns: free-standing columns found in the scan become column instances (structural column families first, else architectural) of their shape, rectangular or round, at their place and rotation, from their level to the storey height; a column's faces are not also walls.
  • G-8.4 (user, 25.11.2026) Railings (balconies, galleries) are built as Revit railings on their path, with the template's railing type and the input's base: straight ones on their axis, bent and curved ones on their centre line (ifc.railings); a railing whose path cannot be read is listed.

9. Rooms

  • G-9.1 One room per enclosed area, placed at a point inside it; name and number from the plan or the IFC, else Room <storey>.<n>; net area to the wall faces. Cells under 1 m2 are slivers, not rooms.
  • G-9.2 A room must be enclosed (Revit: "bounded"), and walls (with their doors and openings) bound it. Room separation lines are used only in two cases (decided by the user, 26.10.2026): 1. A room does not close otherwise: a gap with no wall and no door (a passage, a wide opening, a wall piece the scan did not see) gets one short line across the gap, 5 cm into the wall ends (0.2 m where a scan's wall ends are uncertain); where no outer wall was found (glass fronts, balconies), the building outline closes it. 2. The input divides a space without a wall: the floor plan, IFC or room list has two rooms where no wall stands (a large foyer drawn as "Entrance" and "Foyer"): one line where the input divides them. Never along whole room outlines, never where a wall stands, never more lines than it takes to close the room. Walls close first (user, 25.11.2026: "alle Räume sauber von Wänden umschlossen"): a gap under 0.6 m between two wall pieces on one axis is filled, a wall end up to 1.0 m short of a wall ahead (or of an open corner) is extended to its axis, a gap in the facade is outer wall. Only then a line, straight and on the wall's axis: across a passage between two pieces on one axis (up to 3 m), from a wall end to a wall up to 2.5 m ahead, never traced along a room's free-space boundary and never across a room. At most 20 lines per storey (user, 25.11.2026); a wider gap is a wall the scan did not find: listed, not lined. A room the walls and these lines still do not close is listed for the person (open: walls found there, F1). (26.10.2026: replaces the rule of 25.10.2026 that drew a line along every room edge outside a wall body: a real house got 1240 lines, most of them useless.) Slice route: the lines are drawn only where two free-space rooms, or a room and the outside of the building outline (G-6.5), meet with no wall within 0.2 m, one straightened run per gap, its ends moved 0.2 m into the walls; wall ends closer than 0.45 m get one short line across. (open: long boundaries between rooms where no wall was found still get a line; the house fell from 1240 to about 300 lines on four storeys.)
  • G-9.3 Room stamps of a plan sheet (plans.sheet): every stamp gives a room at its position, also where the walls do not close around it (then placed, not enclosed, listed in qa). Name = the stamp's name ("Küche"); the unit above it goes to Revit's room parameter "Abteilung" (Department): "Wohnung <n>", "G" = "Allgemein"; number = storey.unit.nn ("EG.1.03", counted per storey and unit). On sheets with filled walls every gap in a wall is an opening: a door (swing), a window (frame lines) or, without a symbol, a passage with a room separation line.
  • G-9.6 Rooms are shown in every floor plan of their level: a room tag (Raumstempel) at the room's point, of a type that shows at least the area (a template type whose name says area / Fläche, else the first loaded), and the rooms' interior fill ("Innenfüllung") visible, which Revit draws pale blue on screen (set on the plans' view template where they have one, so it holds for every plan of it; Revit leaves it out of prints and image exports, and its colour is Revit's own). Room data of the input go into the room: name, number, unit (department), use (occupancy) and finishes where the input carries them. (25.10.2026, asked by the user.)

10. Coordinates and georeferencing

  • G-10.1 The model stays near Revit's internal origin; the Survey Point carries the large coordinates (LV95 E, N and the height): plan.origin.offset_xyz. True north: the angle -rotation_deg when --align-north turned the model. Set them in Revit (or tell the user to); never move elements to "fix" coordinates.
  • G-10.2 One coordinate source for all storeys and disciplines (the survey); acquire or publish shared coordinates once, link by shared coordinates, check with a control point.
  • G-10.3 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) Survey control points (three to seven, spread over the building; Autodesk Tandem guidance) and the national frame (LV95, LN02 or LHN95) are recorded in status.md when the survey gives them; the structure man sets the survey point from them and the report names the control point the check used.

12. Checks a model must pass (tools/revit_e2e.py)

G-12.1 Levels at the planned elevations; walls (straight and curved) per level and their thicknesses; walls and floors generic; doors and windows hosted and within 50 mm of their size; window sills; floors per level and their thicknesses; slab openings cut; stairs (risers, tread, levels); rooms bounded and their areas within 3 %; door swings to the scanned side; Revit warnings and the failures answered without a transaction rolled back. Each check names the rule it tests by its id; the whole list, and the rules no check covers yet: section 14.

13. Working rules

  • G-13.1 Never move or edit elements that were not created in this run.
  • G-13.2 Revit errors during a stage: an error that offers a resolution is resolved with Revit's default (walls that cannot stay joined are unjoined) and listed in the stage notes, instead of rolling back the whole stage; a floor whose outline Revit refuses is tried once more with a coarser outline, then left out with a note.
  • G-13.3 Do not create types, levels or coordinates silently in a real project: ask; in test copies the runner does it.
  • G-13.4 Quote accuracy with its source: synthetic results (bench/results.md) are optimistic; real scans carry registration error, glass and clutter.
  • G-13.5 (proposed 26.10.2026, awaiting approval) Every build is recorded with its setup in the build register (scan2revit.core.builds, the site's Builds page): model file, project, inputs, setup id, Revit, pyRevit, template and its hash, the phases and the check result. A build is never overwritten by a later setup: a rebuild (revit_project.py open --rebuild) gets a new file next to the old one, the old one's log and check are kept in builds/. Test models (tools/revit_e2e.py) are kept the same way.
  • G-13.6 (user, 28.11.2026) Missing component families are flagged in the analysis before Revit (gate 1), not found in the build: the template's families (tools/template_families.py, <template>.families.json) against what the plan asks for (rectangular and round columns, doors, windows, the families named by family_hint); the analysis page and quality.md list what the template lacks, the family man loads it or the element is left out and reported, as the user decides at gate 1.
  • G-13.7 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) Before every stage the tool checks that the same Revit session and the same document are open as at the start; otherwise the stage is not run and the run stops (rvt-mcp: target checked before every write).
  • G-13.8 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) A check that could not run is "not verified", never a pass, and does not count as passed (rvt-mcp pre-issue check).
  • G-13.9 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) Revit warnings are findings: a build passes only when every warning is explained (today: the rooms the plan knows to be open); the others go to the agent of the stage (rvt-mcp: warnings roll back by default).
  • G-13.10 (user, 28.11.2026, from the research of open guidelines) No two elements of one category overlap: walls along a straight run (G-5.3), floors over each other on one level, columns inside each other or inside a wall (rvt-mcp audit tools).
